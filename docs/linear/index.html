Linear Regression Outlier Explorer

A linear model, $y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x + \epsilon$ with true coefficients $\beta_0 = 2$ and $\beta_1 = -3$, fitted 50 times over a grid of contamination settings. In each sample a share of the observations is replaced by outliers, whose errors are drawn from $N(\mu_{\epsilon_{\text{out}}}, 1)$, with $\mu_{\epsilon_{\text{out}}}$ running from $-5$ to $5$ in steps of 1. There are three estimators: OLS, ETEL_bias and ETEL. For a given setting and replicate, one dataset is generated, and all three estimators are fitted to that identical dataset. The knobs below choose which sample you are looking at, and every view reads the same choice.

The inner product λ⊤g is what the fit assigns to each observation, and the implied probabilities are its softmax, so the two panels are the same numbers before and after normalising. The dashed rule is the uniform weight 1/N that ordinary least squares gives every point. The bottom panel puts weight against distance from the true line: a downweighting that works is a curve falling away from the centre.

ETEL log-likelihood over (β0, β1)

Profiles through the maximum

Contours of the exponentially tilted empirical likelihood on a grid over the two coefficients. The clay mark is the true (2, −3). The ink mark is the best point on the grid. The window slider clips everything more than that many log-likelihood units below the maximum, which is what keeps the contours near the peak from collapsing into a single blot.
