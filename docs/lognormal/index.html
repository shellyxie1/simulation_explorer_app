Log-normal Outlier Explorer

A log-normal sample, $y_i \sim \mathrm{LN}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with $\mu = 0$ and $\sigma^2 = 0.25$, of size $n = 1000$, drawn 50 times over a grid of contamination settings. In each sample a share of the observations is contaminated: their log-mean is shifted by $\delta$, so $\log y_i \sim N(\mu + \delta, \sigma^2)$, with $\delta$ running over $-4, \dots, -1, 1, \dots, 4$. Two exponentially tilted empirical likelihood (ETEL) fits use the first three raw moments, $E[y^k] = \exp(k\mu + k^2\sigma^2/2)$, as estimating equations: one is started at the true $(\mu, \sigma^2)$, the other from a biased point 70% of the way from the truth to the edge of the feasible region in the direction $(1, 1)$. Both see the same sample. The knobs below choose which sample you are looking at, and every view reads the same choice.

Clean and outlier densities

The dashed rule is the true mean $\exp(\mu + \sigma^2/2)$. Each solid rule is the mean implied by one fit to this sample. In the top-right panel the pale ticks are that fit's other 49 replicates at the same setting. The bottom-right panel is the mixture the sample is drawn from: the clean component $\mathrm{LN}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ and the contaminated component $\mathrm{LN}(\mu + \delta, \sigma^2)$, each as a density on the raw scale. Cumulative share of weight

The inner product λ⊤gi is what the fit assigns to each observation, and the implied probabilities πi are its softmax, so the two panels are the same numbers before and after normalising. The dashed rule is the uniform weight 1/N. A collapsed fit puts almost all weight on a handful of points; that is invisible on the raw scale and obvious on the log scale. The bottom-left panel puts weight against $\log y_i - \mu$, the distance of each point from the true log-mean: a downweighting that works is a curve falling away from zero.

Profiles through the grid maximum

Contours of the exponentially tilted empirical likelihood on a grid over the two parameters. Grey cells are infeasible: the zero vector is outside the convex hull of the moment conditions there, so no tilt exists. The clay mark is the true (0, 0.25); the ink mark is the best point on the grid; the coloured marks are the two ETEL estimates, with a thin line from the biased start to where that optimiser ended. The window slider clips everything more than that many log-likelihood units below the maximum, which is what keeps the contours near the peak from collapsing into a single blot.
