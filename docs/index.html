Outlier Simulation Explorers

What happens to an exponentially tilted empirical likelihood (ETEL) fit when part of the sample is contaminated? Two simulation studies, each with the same four views over the same three knobs. Choose a study.

Linear regression

A simple linear model $y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x + \epsilon$ with true coefficients $\beta = (2, -3)$. In each sample a share of the observations is replaced by outliers, whose errors are drawn from $N(\mu_{\epsilon_{\text{out}}}, 1)$. Three estimators are fitted to the identical dataset: ordinary least squares, ETEL from a biased start, and ETEL started at the truth.

contamination1%, 5%, 10% of the sample
shift$\mu_{\epsilon_{\text{out}}}$ from $-5$ to $5$ in steps of $1$
replicates50 per setting, 1000 observations each

Log-normal distribution

A log-normal sample $y \sim \mathrm{LN}(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with $(\mu, \sigma^2) = (0, 0.25)$, fitted through its first three raw moments $E[y^k] = \exp(k\mu + k^2\sigma^2/2)$. In each sample a share of the observations has its log-mean shifted by $\delta$. Two ETEL fits see the identical dataset: one started at the truth, one from a biased start 70% of the way to the edge of the feasible region.

contamination1%, 5%, 10% of the sample
shift$\delta$ from $-4$ to $4$, skipping $0$
replicates50 per setting, 1000 observations each
